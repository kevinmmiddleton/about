A note from a member

Hi MyFitnessPal team,

I've logged food in MyFitnessPal since 2011. Fifteen years, a few good runs, a few long breaks, and I keep coming back because your database has everything I eat.

Logging is still a chore, though. I have an AI assistant that handles my calendar, my email, and my gym check-ins, and it can't touch the app I open most. When I went looking for a workaround, I found members already building one: an unofficial MCP server that drives your website with a pasted session cookie. It works until your site changes, and it asks people to hand their login to a stranger's code.

Strava hit this same moment and turned it into a reason to subscribe. Buffer shipped theirs in May, and I use Buffer more because of it. MyFitnessPal is a better fit than either, because food gets logged in the moment, on a phone, often mid-bite. Voice Log shows you already believe that. An MCP server takes it to wherever members are already talking.

I sketched the whole thing below: the chat, the diary, the settings screen, and your plans table with two new rows. I'm a product manager, so I'm biased toward thinking this is easy. I know it isn't. I'd love to talk it through.

Kevin MiddletonMember since 2011 · middleton.io

Setup

Connect once. Revoke anytime.

It lives in Settings next to the apps and devices members already connect. Reading is for everyone. Logging is the Premium switch.

  • Real sign-in, no pasted cookies.OAuth with separate read and write permissions, the same pattern your partner integrations use.
  • Every assistant entry is marked."via Claude" or "via ChatGPT" in the diary, with one-tap undo.
  • No double lunches.Each write carries a request ID, so a retried call never logs the same meal twice.
  • The meal is never a guess.If the member didn't say Lunch or Snacks, the assistant asks.

Who's already doing this

Strava made it a subscriber perk. Buffer made it the default.

Two apps members already pay for shipped official MCP servers in 2026. Strava's reasoning reads like it was written about MyFitnessPal.

Subscribers onlyLaunched June 1, 2026Read-only

Strava

"They've been doing it with spreadsheets, exports, and third-party scripts for years. The MCP connector gives them a far more efficient, safer tool while keeping the athlete in control, representing a step-change in our subscription offering."
Ryan Dixon, VP Partnerships & Developer Relations, Strava. Press release

Strava's is read-only: it can't upload or edit activities. For food, writing is the whole point, so MyFitnessPal would ship something Strava hasn't.

Every plan, even FreeLaunched May 27, 2026Can write

Buffer

"That means you can tell your AI agent, 'Schedule three posts promoting this week's blog,' and it will actually do it."
Buffer, "Buffer's API is Open for Building." Announcement · buffer.com/mcp

Buffer's version writes, with a safety net: posts land as drafts, and "nothing publishes without you seeing it first." The "via Claude" tag with undo is the same idea for a food diary.

Also: Todoist runs an official hosted MCP server that creates and updates tasks, and features that are Pro in the app stay Pro through the assistant. That's the packaging model proposed here.

Why MyFitnessPal is ready

Most of the pieces already exist.

Members are already building it

An unofficial MCP server logs food by scraping the website with a pasted session cookie, and python-myfitnesspal reads diaries through browser cookies. That's Strava's "third-party scripts," word for word.

You've already bet on fast logging

Voice Log, Meal Scan, the ChatGPT app, and the Cal AI acquisition all push toward logging with less effort. An MCP server meets members in the assistant they already have open.

The plumbing is there

MyFitnessPal already runs a partner API with OAuth for connected apps. An MCP server is a thin, well-understood layer over the same diary and food search.
