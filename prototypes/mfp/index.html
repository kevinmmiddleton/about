Fifteen years of logging, and it's still the part I put off

I've used MyFitnessPal since 2011. Every assistant I use can reach my calendar, my email, and my to-do list. But I still have to log MFP entries by hand.

Hi MyFitnessPal team,

Your own research this summer found daily engagement was the strongest predictor of success: the most engaged members were more than twice as likely to reach their goal. Allowing members to log from their AI assistant makes a daily tracking habit even easier. And easier habits mean better retention, and a reason to keep paying for Premium.

Members are already building this with an unofficial connector that runs on a pasted session cookie. Strava made it a subscriber perk. Buffer has championed their MCP.

Two subscription apps shipped official MCP servers this year. MCP is the open standard Claude and ChatGPT use to connect to other apps. Strava's reasoning is the closest match to MyFitnessPal's.

press.strava.com/articles/strava-launches-mcp-connector

Strava

  • Launched June 1, 2026, starting with Claude
  • Subscribers only, part of the Strava subscription
  • Their VP of Partnerships called it "a step-change in our subscription offering"

Strava's connector is read-only: it can't upload or edit activities. For food, writing is the whole point, so MyFitnessPal would ship something Strava hasn't.

Buffer

Buffer put its MCP server on every plan, even Free, and it writes. Posts land in the queue for review first, because "nothing publishes without you seeing it first."

Why not free, like Buffer? Buffer's writes are drafts that a person reviews before anything posts. In MyFitnessPal the log is the product, and fast logging is already what Premium sells: Voice Log and Meal Scan are both Premium. Reading stays free, so everyone can try it.

Most of the pieces already exist

An official MCP server is a thin layer on top of work MyFitnessPal has already done. Here is what is in place, and the question a CPO will ask first.

Members are already building it

An unofficial MCP server logs food by scraping the website with a pasted session cookie, and python-myfitnesspal reads diaries through browser cookies.

You've already bet on fast logging

Voice Log, Meal Scan, the ChatGPT app, and the Cal AI acquisition all push toward logging with less effort. An MCP server gives members one more way to log that works in whatever assistant they already use.

The sign-in model exists

MyFitnessPal's existing partner integrations already connect outside apps to a member's account with scoped permissions. An MCP server uses the same model for assistants.

Privacy stays with the member

Health data only reaches an assistant after the member connects it, with the permissions they pick, and they can revoke it in one tap.

Everything members (and their assistants) need at their fingertips.

Anyone can search foods and check daily progress. Premium members can log, edit, and undo.

Fifteen years in, I'd still renew for this

A place to start: an open beta for every member. Anyone can connect an assistant and ask about their diary; logging from it is Premium. Watch logged days per week and renewal for members who connect, how many free members upgrade after they try to log, and how many new accounts start from an assistant.
