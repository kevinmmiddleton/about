Personalize from what customers tell you and what they buy, never from guesses.

How we learn more

Ask when the answer is fresh

A gift toggle at checkout. A tag on Buy Again: mine, a gift, someone at home. One tap each.

Say thanks with a pair, not a discount

Tag your purchases and we'll donate an extra pair. Bombas has no loyalty program, and impact already brings people back. A discount for data teaches people to wait for discounts.

Who were these for? Tag 3 purchases and we'll donate one more pair.

Read the gift clues already in the order

A ship-to address that isn't theirs. A gift message. A gift box.

Guesses can hide things. Only what the customer confirms can change what we show them.

The other half of every gift

The person who got the gift is a new customer

Every gift box lands with someone who may never have bought Bombas. A card in the box lets them save their size, so their first order fits. The giver is a retention moment. The person opening the box is an acquisition one. That's where part one picks up.

    4 · The north star, and what moves it

    before any of this

    Define "meaningful" first

    The north star counts visits that return to a "meaningful product interaction." Settle what that means with the team first. It decides what the number rewards. My draft:

    Counts

    • a product page view
    • a Buy Again tap or add to bag
    • a fit answer saved to the customer's profile
    • a help issue resolved without a contact

    Doesn't count

    • a login that goes nowhere
    • opening an email
    • landing on the homepage and leaving
    north starSessions returning to a meaningful product interaction within 30 days of a purchase
    the ai cohort audit

    I did this by hand for one customer. AI can do it for 500.

    My audit lined up every message I got against what I'd bought. The same check, run across a real group of customers, turns one story into a pattern, and the pattern into rules worth testing.

    1. 1Pick a groupAbout 500 recent customers, store and online.
    2. 2CompareAI reads each customer's purchases against every message they got. Clicks and visits count. Opens don't, since Apple Mail inflates them.
    3. 3Write the rulesSkip what they own. Slow down for people who don't click. Keep gifts out of "your size."
    4. 4Test on a fresh groupThe new rules against today's emails, on customers we didn't study. Written up either way.
    The testtoday's emails versus the same calendar with the audit's rules, for half of new store and online customersHow we'd knowthe north star itself: more sessions returning to a meaningful product interaction within 30 daysWhat we'd watchrevenue per email and unsubscribes, so the calmer plan doesn't cost us the emails that work

    Retention & CRMthe emails themselves, in Braze
    Analyticsthe readout
    AIthe audit, and applying rules across thousands of customers. Less so the copy.

    • It produces learning in weeks. The team says it has few learnings in this space so far.
    • Light engineering. A just-bought list, sent to Braze.
    • It moves repeat rate, and the north star with it.
    • It starts the CRM partnership on day one.

    Questions for the team

    first betWhat first big bet do you and the Head of Digital Product have in mind, and what have you already learned in this space?
    measurementWhat counts as a "meaningful product interaction" today? Is a fit answer or a resolved help issue in, or only purchases?
    crmHow do Retention, CRM, and product plan sends together today? Is skipping just-bought products a choice, or just not built yet?
    engineeringWhich customer and order data can the site use live, and what has to be worked out ahead of time?
    storesThe store team sends thoughtful follow-ups through Endear. Do customer replies reach the account or CRM, and do store customers know their orders are already online?
    checkoutDoes checkout already ask whether an order is a gift? That answer should tag the purchase, so customers never have to.
