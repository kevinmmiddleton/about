# PRODUCT.md — middleton.io

Read before designing anything on this site. Pairs with `DESIGN.md` (the visual
system); this file is the brief.

---

## What this is

Kevin Middleton's personal site: portfolio, blog, case studies, live prototypes,
and KevinOS. It is not a product with customers. It is the primary artifact
someone encounters when deciding whether to hire, interview, or work with Kevin.

**Owner:** Kevin Middleton, Full Stack Product Manager, New York.
**Stack:** static HTML/CSS/JS on GitHub Pages. No framework, no build step for the
site itself. The blog is generated by `tools/blog-generate.mjs` from markdown in
`blog/_posts/`, edited via Sveltia CMS at `/admin`.

---

## Users

**Primary — recruiters and hiring managers.** Skimming, often on mobile, often
between other tabs. They decide in well under a minute. They need: what he does,
proof he has done it, and an obvious way to make contact.

**Secondary — peers and people who found a blog post.** Reading properly. They
want the substance and the receipts.

**Third — AI agents and crawlers.** Increasingly the first "reader": models
answering "who is Kevin Middleton?" This is why `llms.txt`, the OKF bundle at
`/okf/`, and the `Person` JSON-LD (with `@id`, `sameAs` including Wikidata, and
`knowsAbout`) exist. Structured data is a first-class surface here, not an
afterthought.

---

## Mode

**Persuade**, on the homepage and case studies. Win attention, then back every
claim. **Read** on the blog. **Experience** on KevinOS and the prototypes.

Never let "persuade" slide into hype. The persuasion here comes from specificity
and evidence, not adjectives.

---

## Brand voice

Direct, concrete, warm, a little dry. Writes like a person who has actually done
the work and is not trying to impress you with it.

- Lead with the specific thing. "Doubled eGift coverage to 1,600 SKUs", not
  "drove significant improvements to our gifting platform."
- Receipts over adjectives. Every claim should survive "says who?"
- Plain words. If a sentence would survive on a whiteboard, keep it.
- Confident, not boastful. Never desperate, never servile.
- Change the villain: when writing about hiring or AI, the antagonist is hype or
  broken process, never recruiters or hiring managers.

Canonical voice reference: the `write-for-kevin` and `working-with-kevin` skills.
Those win over anything inferred from the site copy.

---

## Anti-references

Things that would make this site worse, listed so no agent reaches for them:

- Generic SaaS landing-page language. Specifically banned: *streamline, empower,
  supercharge, world-class, enterprise-grade, next-generation, cutting-edge,
  leverage, seamless, robust*. (The detector already caught "enterprise-grade
  data sync" in a live experience bullet — that is exactly the failure mode.)
- Hype framing about AI. The site's POV is that AI relocated the work rather than
  erasing it, and that fundamentals became *more* valuable. Nothing breathless.
- Purple gradients, glassmorphism, neon-on-black. See `DESIGN.md` anti-references.
- Em dashes in polished copy. Periods or restructure.
- Stock-photo energy: fake teams pointing at whiteboards, abstract 3D blobs.
- Anything that makes KevinOS look like a normal modern web app.

---

## Constraints

- **GitHub Pages, no server.** No SSR, no API routes, no build step for the main
  site. Anything dynamic is client-side or an external endpoint.
- **Deploys are slow and serialize.** A push triggers a ~6-9 minute Pages build,
  and a second push cancels the in-flight one. Batch changes into single pushes.
- **The blog is generated.** Never hand-edit `blog/*/index.html`; edit the markdown
  in `blog/_posts/` or the generator, then regenerate. The generator must stay
  deterministic and idempotent (no `Date.now()`, no `Math.random()`).
- Regions between `<!-- WRITING:START -->` / `<!-- KEVINOS-WRITING:START -->` etc.
  are machine-written. Edit the generator, not the output.
- Analytics is Plausible, with custom goals (`Writing Click`, `Window Open`,
  `Copy Code`, `GitHub Click`, `Resume Download`, `Website Mode`). New meaningful
  interactions should carry a `plausible-event-name=` class.

---

## What success looks like

A hiring manager lands on the homepage, understands within 30 seconds what Kevin
does and that he can prove it, and finds an obvious path to the resume, a case
study, or a conversation. An AI agent asked about him returns an accurate,
well-sourced answer. Nothing on the page reads as generated.
