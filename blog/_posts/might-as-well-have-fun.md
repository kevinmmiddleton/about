My philosophy on life is simple: if I have to do it anyway, I might as well have fun doing it.

It colors almost everything I do. I updated my website because it was fun _and_ practical. I once walked around the boroughs placing 70 QR stickers that sent people to it. I run free office hours. None of it was part of a grand plan. I just like making things, and I'd rather make them than wait around.

It's a throwback, really. Back in January I wrote that I call myself a [seriously unserious](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7415029966599389184/) product manager. I take the work seriously. We just don't have to be so serious all the time.

Here's what I've learned from doing it for a while.

### Make it for yourself first

Build the thing because you find it useful. Write the post because you have something to say. Share it however you like, on LinkedIn or on a blog, and stop caring whether it gets likes. It probably won't get many, and that's okay.

When you make something for yourself first, the bar is whether it helped you, and you can clear that bar every time. Hopefully someone else finds it helpful too. Then keep doing it.

### It gives you something to do

This is the part people skip. Doing things occupies you.

If you're in a lull or between things, the days can blur together. Building something gives each one a point. You wake up with a next step. You have a small problem to chew on during your walk. That alone is worth a lot.

### Fill the gap on purpose

Most of us have a gap somewhere: a skill a job asks for that we haven't used yet, or a subject we want to know better.

You don't have to accept the gap. You can fill it the most enjoyable way you can think of. Build something in your area of weakness and post about it. Write about the subject while you learn it. Team up with other people who want to learn it too.

With AI tools it has never been easier. You don't need permission or a budget. You need a little time and something you want to build.

### The one-minute answer

Recruiters and hiring managers have asked me "what have you been working on?" several times, and I love that question.

I walk through my work experience, and then I add, "and here's what I've been building lately." Usually I share my screen, pull up my own portfolio (which I also built, and had fun building), and show them a few things.

It might take one minute. In that minute they see that you bring your experience and your enthusiasm, and that you're actively building something right now. That leaves a great impression, and it's a lot more fun to talk about than a gap.

### Other people notice, and those are your people

My office hours started as something I made from nothing. GitHub Pages is free. Calendly is free. I put up a page and a booking link, and I've since met dozens of people through it.

I get feedback on the things I'm building. I help people think through their problems, which keeps me sharp and current and teaches me new ways to dig into problems of my own. These are people I'll keep in touch with for years, people I can lean on and trade feedback with.

It keeps happening. This week a senior analyst who'd been searching for almost a year messaged me after one of my posts. He'd been using AI to tailor every resume, and he could tell the results read like fluff. We worked through his resume together. Neither of us was selling anything. He found me because I'd shared something I made.

When you make things and share them, the right people find you.

### Networking that works

Most people only network when they're in danger. They reach out the week they need a job, and it feels transactional because it is.

What if we all just networked all the time? That's how networking is truly meant to be: a regular way to talk with people. Saving it for emergencies is what makes it feel awkward. Office hours turned networking into something I enjoy, so I actually do it. I found a way to make networking work for me, pun intended.

### So build something

Pick something you want to make. Make it for yourself first. Share it without worrying about the likes. Keep going.

Someone will notice. Those are your people.
