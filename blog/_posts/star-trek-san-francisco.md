In my last post, [Marvel warned us about AI](https://middleton.io/blog/no-more-ai/), Wanda said "No more mutants," and Tony Stark picked San Francisco to beta-test his Extremis 3.0 app. That's the version of the future I'd like to skip.

But there's another fictional San Francisco I keep thinking about. It's the one in Star Trek, home of Starfleet, where the Golden Gate Bridge is still standing hundreds of years from now. **In that San Francisco, it went right.**

### Hello, computer

One of my favorite scenes is from _Star Trek IV: The Voyage Home_. The crew travels back in time to 1986 San Francisco, and Scotty sits down at a Macintosh. He picks up the mouse and talks into it: "Hello, computer."

![Scotty from Star Trek IV picks up a Macintosh mouse and speaks into it like a microphone, with the caption "Hello, computer."](/blog/images/star-trek-hello-computer.gif "Scotty, 1986 San Francisco: \"Hello, computer.\"")

It's funny because Scotty is used to a computer that just listens. On the Enterprise, you say "Computer" out loud and it truly helps you. Nobody's tapping through menus or babysitting a chatbot. The crew asks for what they need, and the humans still make the calls.

My favorite part of Star Trek, and I think what a lot of people want, is that "Computer."

### Right now, AI feels like hard work

AI today feels like hard work. We spend more time with it. We prompt, we check, we prompt again, and we check again.

But sometimes I notice I spend **_less_** time with AI. My morning briefing goes through my email, so I don't have to. I get warned about things proactively, instead of finding out later. In those moments, AI feels like the Enterprise computer: it shows up when I need it, and then it gets out of the way.

I can see my friends more now, because my agent helps me find activities, keeps me from overbooking myself, and helps me plan. It takes tedious tasks off my plate. The more proactive agents are getting better at helping us spend less time in front of a screen, so we can spend more face time.

I'm still finding my balance. I do notice I'm on my phone less often, though part of that might be that I traded it for a bit of laptop time. Then again, I'm writing more, building more, and doing more of my hobbies, some of which do involve screens. In that way, I am happier. I think the next step is to take up a craft or trade that's decidedly not tech.

That's a positive outcome. That's what people want.

### What Star Trek's San Francisco got right

In Star Trek, people do the jobs they want, and they use technology when it suits them, without being totally entrenched in it. And it shows. People are into the arts, and they pursue jobs that interest them. On _Deep Space Nine_, Captain Sisko's dad runs a restaurant in New Orleans and cooks by hand, even though a replicator could make anything.

Food isn't scarce. There's universal healthcare. Homelessness has been solved.

The show even knew where we were starting from. In the _Deep Space Nine_ episode "Past Tense," the crew lands in San Francisco in 2024, where unhoused people are walled off in "Sanctuary Districts." That episode aired in 1995. Star Trek imagined a rough San Francisco in our time, and then a future where people fixed it.

I think it goes without saying that profit motives didn't ruin that society. People did the right thing.

So what's the opposite of the Star Trek future? _Wall-E_ might be a bit too cartoonish, but you get the idea: everyone glued to a screen while the machines run everything.

I think there's going to be a backlash. When you think about the response to data centers, I still think they're going to get built, but the public is getting exhausted, especially when we want our lives to be easier and the use cases aren't there yet.

### Be intentional

Star Trek's San Francisco didn't happen by accident. Somebody decided food, healthcare, and a home were for everyone, and that technology should serve people.

We get to make the same choice with AI. Build ambient computing that helps without asking for our attention all day. Think about the societal impact before we ship. Use technology for good over profits. If we take those lessons, we can get it right.

But that's a big if.

Marvel showed us the AI future to avoid. Star Trek showed us the one worth building. **Which San Francisco are we building?**
