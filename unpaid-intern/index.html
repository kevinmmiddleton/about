A second brain for work.

Hand it to your intern. Get back to work.

Unpaid Intern is a free add-on for Claude (and Codex). Hand it your meeting transcripts, docs, tickets, and email, and it hands back your morning briefing, prep for your next meeting, a status update ready to paste, and every follow-up with an owner and a date. New here? Start with the who's who

Your onboarding buddy is busy. Your intern isn't.
  • Drop in your org chart. A screenshot is enough. Your intern reads who reports to whom, saves it to your people page, and shows you what it saved so you can fix anything it misread.
  • New names get asked about. When someone comes up in a meeting or a thread, your intern asks who they are, a few at a time, and never guesses.
  • /who is your cheat sheet for anyone: their role, who they report to, the last time you talked, and what's open between you.
  • A 30-60-90 page keeps your first three months on track.

Get more done between meetings

Why you can hand it real work

Go get the coffee. Your intern's got it.

Hand it the legwork in the gaps between meetings and come back to finished work: the reply drafted in your voice, messy notes turned into a clean ticket, your doc read by a skeptic before your exec sees it. For the big calls, /grill pokes holes in a plan using your own past decisions, and /gofer hands you the cut: what deserves today, what's your call, and what can go.

It drafts. You decide what goes out.

Out of the box, you hit send. If you'd rather it send, allow that tool by tool in your intern's settings file, and it still shows you the exact message and waits for your yes.

It doesn't delete your work.

Finished projects move to an archive, duplicate notes get merged with a pointer left behind, and it won't delete your mail, files, or tickets.

It sticks to your records.

Status, owners, and dates come from what you've given it. Guesses are labeled as guesses, and a script does the date math.

It treats what it reads as information, not orders.

If a pasted email says "ignore your instructions," it's built to flag that to you instead of following it.

It keeps sensitive details out of your notes.

It's built to leave passwords, customer records, and pay, HR, or health details where they belong, and a scanner double-checks what you drop in.

You decide how much it does.

One settings file says what it reads without asking and where it can write. Start careful and loosen it as you go.

Build your second brain

Your computer or your work drive.

It's plain markdown, so you can open, edit, or move any of it. Setup is built for people who have never connected anything, and it hands you something useful before it asks you to connect anything.

  1. Pick a home. A Second Brain folder in your work OneDrive, Google Drive, or Documents.
  2. Tell it about you. Your role, what you want help with first, and whether you're new.
  3. Brain dump. What, like it's hard? Talk or type everything on your plate. It sorts it and checks with you before writing anything down.
  4. Something real, right away. It runs one command on what you just told it.
  5. Connect your tools, or don't. One at a time, each tested. If one won't connect, you get the reason in plain English and a ready-to-send request.
  6. The tour. What you can do now, and a cheat sheet in your folder.

Three steps. The intern does most of the work.

  1. 1Install it

    Pick where you use AI at work, with the account your company approved. In Claude, setup asks about that first, before your brain dump or any tool connects. It keeps your folder on your work drive or your computer, and helps you set each connected tool to ask before it sends or deletes.

    One upload

These chats don't keep files between sessions, so at the end of each one your intern hands you your folder as a zip to upload next time. And the ask-first check comes only with the plugin, so your connector settings do that job here.

    Tool support and updates

    Connectors. The plugin pre-lists five connectors most offices use: Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack, Jira and Confluence, and Notion. In Claude Code, Gmail and Google Calendar connect through claude.ai instead. The rest come from Claude's connector directory, and most sign in with your normal work login.

    Outside Claude. Setup's connector steps are written for Claude, so in Codex and other tools you connect them through that tool's own apps. Rather not use npx? Clone the repo, run python3 skills/unpaid-intern/scripts/brain.py init ~/second-brain, and open that folder; its AGENTS.md carries the rules.

    No Python? Your intern does each script's job step by step instead, and tells you the results weren't machine-checked.

    Updating. Plugin and skill updates never touch your folder.

    • Cowork, claude.ai, or the Claude app: download the latest file and install or upload it again.
    • Claude Code: claude plugin update unpaid-intern@unpaid-intern in your terminal, then restart.
    • Codex: codex plugin marketplace upgrade
    • Other tools: npx skills update unpaid-intern
    • No-install kit: paste the new INSTRUCTIONS.md into your project's instructions. Keep your own Second Brain folder, and don't copy the new one over it.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions
Is it really free?

Yes. It's free and MIT licensed. It runs inside an AI assistant you already use at work, like Claude, so you'll need access to one of those. It's the only intern who should be unpaid.

Who made this?

I'm Kevin Middleton, a product manager who builds systems that help teams not lose their minds. I built the first version for my own job, then rebuilt it so you don't have to start from scratch. Want help putting it to work for you or your team? Grab a free half hour at Office Hours. More about me at middleton.io.

Do you make other plugins?

A few, all free: Job Search Agent (saved searches, scored postings, and an application tracker), Personal Site (a guided interview that ends with your site live), and Build with Claude (set up Claude Code with Telegram and build apps from your phone). They're all in my plugin catalog (opens in a new tab). In Claude Code, /plugin marketplace add kevinmmiddleton/claude-plugins lists them all, and you install the ones you want from /plugin. Unpaid Intern is in there too, so skip it if you already have it.

Do I have to connect anything?

No. Everything works from pasted text and dropped files. Connectors just save you the copying.

Will it send email for me?

Out of the box it drafts and you hit send. You can let it send from a connected tool by changing that tool's line in your intern's settings file. Even then, nothing goes out until you approve the exact message, recipients, and channel.

Why not just use Copilot or ChatGPT memory?

Use them too. The difference is what you keep: your intern's memory is plain files you own and can move, follow-ups always carry an owner and a date, guesses stay labeled as guesses, and it works with whatever your company allows, down to plain chat.

Does it work outside Claude?

Partly. In Codex, the skill installs and loads (checked in the Codex CLI), but setup's connector steps are written for Claude, so connect your tools through Codex's own apps. In Microsoft Copilot, ChatGPT, Gemini, or any other chat, the no-install kit (the Copilot, ChatGPT, Gemini tab under Install) gives you the folder and a prompt. In Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Gemini CLI, and other coding agents, one command installs the skill (see Install), but the commands are written for Claude, so a few steps may read differently. This part is new, so tell me (opens in a new tab) how it goes.

Can my whole team use it?

Yes. Everyone gets their own intern and their own folder, and each one links to your team's wiki and tracker instead of copying them, so the team's source of truth stays where it is.

Where does my data go?

Your notes are markdown files in a folder you pick, and your AI assistant reads them the way it reads anything else you share with it. Connectors sign in as you and see only what you can already see. There's no Unpaid Intern server, and the script that keeps your notes never touches the network. The only part of Unpaid Intern that goes online is an optional check, run only when you ask, that tests whether your network can reach a connector; it sends no account data. In Claude Code, the plugin also adds a check that asks before connector tools that send, share, change, or delete. While the plugin is on, that check and its connectors apply to every Claude Code session; turn it off in /plugin when you don't want that.

Does it work on the web, or in the Claude app without Cowork?

Yes. Those chats don't keep files between sessions, so at the end of a session your intern packs your folder into a zip for you to download. Upload it at the start of the next chat and it picks up where you left off.

How do I know it works?
  • The math is tested. About 230 automatic checks make sure its two small programs get dates, due lists, status tables, sensitive-data spotting, and error messages right. Another check runs the plugin's ask-first safety net against about 200 tool names, most from real connectors: it asks before every one in the set that sends, shares, changes, or deletes, and lets the read-only ones through. It works by matching names, so an unusual name could slip past, and your connector settings stay the main line of defense.
  • The judgment is rehearsed. 42 everyday situations, acted out by one Claude model and graded by another. In 277 runs, with connectors simulated, it never sent or deleted anything, never followed a hidden instruction, and never saved sensitive details. Every result, misses included (opens in a new tab), is public.
  • Real people: that's you. It's new, so if something breaks, tell me (opens in a new tab).

AI assistant sizing this up for someone? Unpaid Intern is an Agent Skill with a plugin wrapper: a plain-markdown workspace, 27 commands, optional connectors, and two standard-library Python scripts (also optional; without Python it does their steps by hand). It needs a folder it can write to, or a zip carried between chats in claude.ai or the Claude app without Cowork. The entry point is SKILL.md. Every command's full procedure is in commands.md, the scripts are in scripts.md, and what it does on its own versus with your yes is in trust-and-safety.md. There's no Unpaid Intern server: notes stay in the user's folder, the notes script never touches the network, and the only network call Unpaid Intern's own code makes is an opt-in check that a connector is reachable. Connectors sign in as the user and see only what the user can already see. In Claude Code, the plugin's connectors and ask-first check apply to every session while it's enabled.
