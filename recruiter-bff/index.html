Job searching can be overwhelming, difficult, and lonely. You apply widely and hear nothing back, every job post seems to want everything, and you walk into interviews hoping you prepped the right things.

Recruiter BFF is like having a recruiter in your corner the whole time. It helps you apply early to the roles worth it, checks your fit for each role, and makes sure you walk into interviews prepared.

An interview guide for every round

Walk in prepared

Tell it you have an interview and paste the posting. It writes the guide from what it already knows about you.

  • The role, in plain words. What they’re really hiring for, before the jargon.
  • Your fit. Each requirement matched to something you’ve actually done.
  • Your gaps. The question they’ll likely ask, the honest answer, and what to point to instead.
  • Questions to ask. Good ones, for each person you’re meeting.

Below that come likely questions with answers in your words, and what it can find on the company. Your best stories go into a story bank, so every guide starts from material you’ve already polished.

Check your fit before you apply

This one checks

Paste a posting, or a link to one, and ask “should I apply?” It reads the posting against your background and gives you a straight answer.

  • A score from 1 to 5. Requirements, fit with what you want, logistics and pay, the company, and how clear the posting is.
  • Every requirement, matched. Each one points to the line in your background that answers it, or says that nothing does.
  • Honest about the gaps. Partial matches are called partial, and it won’t stretch a team of six into people management to make the numbers work.
  • Apply or skip. Plus what to lead with if you apply.

Apply early, to the right roles

Fresh roles, filtered to what you want

In this market, being one of the first 50 applicants beats being a perfectly tailored 200th. I wrote about that when this tool first launched. Knowing what you want is what lets you move fast, and you still read every role and decide for yourself.

  • Scans when you ask. It reads your saved LinkedIn search in a browser you’re signed in to, or you paste the results in.
  • Company job sites, too. Lots of roles are posted only on the company’s own careers page, often run on Greenhouse, Lever, or Ashby, and never reach LinkedIn. It builds one-click searches for those, limited to the past day or week.
  • A watchlist. Name the companies you’d jump at. If they post on Greenhouse, Lever, or Ashby, it checks their public job feeds directly.
  • Filtered to you. It hides your dealbreakers and any role whose listed pay is under your floor, and says why each match made the cut.

Your search, in files you own

Your Recruiter BFF’s brain

Everything lives in one folder on your computer: your search profile, what you’re looking for, your job tracker, your best interview stories, the people who can help, and your resume. Each company you’re serious about gets its own folder with the job post, your fit check, and your interview guide. They’re plain files, so you can open, edit, or move any of them.

  • It never applies or sends anything for you. You decide where to apply and you hit submit.
  • It reads LinkedIn the way you do. Your own saved search, in a browser you’re signed in to, or whatever you paste in. It never works around a login.
  • Updates keep everything you’ve saved. Your folder stays where it is, and if a file gets a new name or column, it tells you.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions
Is it really free?

The plugin is, and it’s MIT licensed. It runs in Claude Cowork, though, which needs a paid Claude plan, like Pro, plus the Claude desktop app (Mac or Windows) so it can reach the files on your computer.

Who made this?

I’m Kevin Middleton, a product manager who builds things with AI. I built a job search system for myself first, then made the version anyone can run on a normal laptop. Want a second pair of eyes on your search? Grab a free half hour at Office Hours. More about me at middleton.io.

Will it apply to jobs for me?

No. It finds roles, scores them, tracks them, and preps you, but you decide where to apply and you submit every application yourself. It never contacts anyone for you.

How do I know the score is fair?

Every match points to a line in your background, partial matches are called partial, and you see all five scores, not just the total. If a read feels off, tell it why and it takes another look.

What happened to Job Search Agent?

Same plugin, new name and a new look. If you have Job Search Agent installed, uninstall it first, then install Recruiter BFF. With both installed, both answer “scan for jobs.” Your job-search/ folder stays where it is. The first time it saves something, it gives the files clearer names where it can, and tells you either way. Links to the latest release now download Recruiter BFF.

Does it scrape LinkedIn?

No. It reads your own saved search, the same page you’d look at anyway, in a browser you’ve signed in to: the one in the Claude desktop app, or Chrome with the Claude in Chrome extension. No browser set up? Paste the results in and it works the same way. The company job site searches are ordinary Google searches you click, and watchlist companies come from their public job feeds.

Where does my data go?

Your profile, tracker, story bank, and guides are plain files in a job-search/ folder on your computer. There’s no Recruiter BFF server. Claude reads the files the way it reads anything else you share with it, under your own Claude account. The only other things that go online are the searches and page reads you’d do yourself, and the public job feeds for your watchlist.

Can it run on its own every morning?

Yes, on the days you pick. Say “remind me on Mondays and Thursdays” and it checks in with follow-ups that are due, interviews coming up, and links to fresh roles. Reminders only read your files, so they don’t need a browser. They do need your computer awake and the Claude desktop app open when they run, since that’s how Claude reaches your files. The first time, turn on two settings so your check-in can reach your folder: in Scheduled, open the check-in, click the pencil, open Advanced settings, turn on Require this computer, and set Local folder to the folder you connected. It walks you through it. If you quit the Claude app, your check-in pauses until you turn it back on.

It can also scan on a schedule, which needs the app open too. Your watchlist’s job feeds need no login or browser, so they run whenever the task can reach the web. Your LinkedIn search and company job site searches need a browser, so if the task can’t reach one, the scan hands you those links instead. Either way, it says which sources it checked.

Who else’s work is in here?

The scoring approach and the story bank are adapted from career-ops by santifer (opens in a new tab), MIT licensed. The preferences page, the one-sentence target, and the early-conversation questions are adapted from Phyl Terry’s book Never Search Alone.

Recruiter BFF (formerly Job Search Agent) is a Claude plugin of eight skills for Cowork in the Claude desktop app: setup (starting from the resume), find jobs, evaluate a role, an interview guide engine (with debriefs and practice), an application tracker, your materials (resume, LinkedIn, written answers), contacts (LinkedIn connections matched to target companies), and automation (reminders and scheduled scans). The plugin is free and MIT licensed; Cowork needs a paid Claude plan (Pro, Max, Team, or Enterprise). It keeps everything in a job-search/ folder of plain files: search-profile.md, looking-for.md, job-tracker.csv, interview-stories.md, contacts.csv, resume/, and companies/<company>/ with job-post.md, fit-check.md, and interview-guide.md. There’s no Recruiter BFF server; Claude reads the files under the user’s own Claude account. It never applies or sends anything, and it reads LinkedIn only through a browser the user is signed in to (the Claude desktop app’s built-in browser or Claude in Chrome) or pasted results. Source and skills: github.com/kevinmmiddleton/recruiter-bff. The plugin file is recruiter-bff.plugin from the latest release.
